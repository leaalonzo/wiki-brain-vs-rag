# Archimedean Spiral

## Definition
The Archimedean spiral is a type of spiral named after the ancient Greek mathematician Archimedes. It is defined by the equation \( r = a + b\theta \) in polar coordinates, where \( r \) is the radial distance from the origin, \( \theta \) is the angular coordinate, and \( a \) and \( b \) are real numbers that determine the size and spacing of the spiral's arms. Unlike logarithmic spirals, the Archimedean spiral has arms that are equidistant from each other, making it a linear spiral.

## Key Mechanisms
The Archimedean spiral is characterized by its uniform spacing between turns, which is a result of the linear relationship between the radius and the angle. This property makes it useful in various applications, such as in the design of spiral antennas, scroll compressors, and in the modeling of certain biological patterns. In computational models, the Archimedean spiral can be used to represent timelines or trajectories that require consistent spacing over time or space.

## Evidence Base
The concept of the Archimedean spiral has been applied in the paper "TeAST: Temporal Knowledge Graph Embedding via Archimedean Spiral Timeline." This paper introduces a novel approach to temporal knowledge graph embedding by utilizing the Archimedean spiral to map relations onto a timeline. The spiral's properties allow for the simultaneous occurrence of relations to be effectively represented, maintaining the static nature of entities while allowing relations to evolve over time. This approach transforms the embedding task into a third-order tensor completion problem, simplifying the representation of temporal dynamics in knowledge graphs.

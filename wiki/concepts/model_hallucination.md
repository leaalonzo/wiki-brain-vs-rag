## Definition
Model hallucination refers to the phenomenon where a language model generates information or responses that are not grounded in the input data or factual reality. This can result in outputs that are plausible-sounding but incorrect or fabricated.

## Context
Model hallucination is a significant challenge in the field of natural language processing, particularly in the development and deployment of large language models (LLMs) for tasks such as question answering, summarization, and dialogue systems. Hallucination occurs when models produce outputs that deviate from factual accuracy, which can undermine the reliability and trustworthiness of AI systems.

The paper "Interleaving Retrieval with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Knowledge-Intensive Multi-Step Questions" highlights an approach called IRCoT, which aims to reduce model hallucination. By interleaving retrieval steps with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, IRCoT improves the factual accuracy of multi-step question answering by guiding the retrieval process with CoT reasoning and vice versa. This method has been shown to enhance retrieval accuracy and reduce factual errors in reasoning, thereby mitigating hallucination.

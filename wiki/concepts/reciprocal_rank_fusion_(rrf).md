## Definition
Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) is an information retrieval technique used to combine the results from multiple ranked lists of documents. It assigns a score to each document based on its rank in each list, with higher-ranked documents receiving higher scores. The scores from all lists are then aggregated to produce a final ranking, which can improve retrieval accuracy by leveraging the strengths of different retrieval methods.

## Context
RRF is particularly useful in domains where multiple retrieval strategies are employed, such as lexical and semantic retrieval methods. By integrating results from diverse approaches, RRF can enhance the overall retrieval performance. This technique is relevant in specialized fields like electronic design automation (EDA), where domain-specific knowledge is crucial for accurate information retrieval. In the context of retrieval augmented generation (RAG) systems, RRF can be employed to improve the quality of document retrieval, which in turn enhances the effectiveness of downstream tasks like question-answering.

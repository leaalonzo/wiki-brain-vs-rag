## Definition
LLM factuality refers to the accuracy and truthfulness of the information generated by large language models (LLMs). It assesses how well these models can produce correct and reliable answers, particularly in the context of rapidly changing information and potential false premises.

## Context
The factuality of LLMs is a critical aspect of their utility, especially as they are increasingly used in applications requiring up-to-date and precise information. The paper "FRESHLLMS: Refreshing Large Language Models with Search Engine Augmentation" introduces FRESHQA, a dynamic QA benchmark designed to evaluate LLM factuality against current world knowledge. This benchmark categorizes questions into never-changing, slow-changing, fast-changing, and false-premise to test LLMs' ability to handle different types of information.

The study highlights that LLMs often struggle with fast-changing knowledge and false-premise questions, leading to inaccuracies or hallucinations—instances where the model generates information that is not grounded in reality. To address these challenges, the paper proposes FRESHPROMPT, a method that enhances LLM performance by integrating search engine results into prompts, thereby significantly improving factuality.

# Generative Re-Ranking

## Definition
Generative re-ranking is a process in information retrieval systems where initially retrieved documents or passages are re-evaluated and re-ordered using generative models. This approach aims to enhance the relevance of the results by leveraging the capabilities of neural models to understand and generate text, thereby providing a more nuanced assessment of the relevance of each document to a given query.

## Key Mechanisms
Generative re-ranking typically involves the following steps:
1. **Initial Retrieval**: A set of candidate documents or passages is retrieved using traditional retrieval methods, such as sparse retrieval (e.g., BM25) or dense retrieval techniques.
2. **Generative Model Application**: A generative model, often a large-scale transformer-based model like T5, is used to re-rank these candidates. The model generates relevance scores based on its understanding of the query and the content of each document.
3. **Re-ranking**: The candidates are re-ordered according to the scores generated by the model, with the goal of placing the most relevant documents at the top of the list.

## Evidence Base
The concept of generative re-ranking is explored in the paper "Hybrid Retrievers with Generative Re-Rankers" by M. Kozłowski, presented at PolEval 2022. In this study, a multi-stage neural information retrieval system is introduced, combining sparse and dense retrieval methods with a generative re-ranking process. The system uses the mt5-13b-mmarco model for re-ranking, demonstrating significant improvements in the relevance of retrieved passages. The paper highlights the effectiveness of this approach in enhancing passage retrieval performance, particularly in the context of Polish-language datasets.

## Definition
Hallucination in Large Language Models (LLMs) refers to the generation of incorrect or fabricated information by these models. This phenomenon occurs when LLMs produce responses that are not grounded in factual data or real-world knowledge, often due to limitations in their training data or the dynamic nature of world information.

## Context
The issue of hallucination is particularly prevalent when LLMs handle fast-changing information or questions based on false premises. Traditional LLMs, which rely on static datasets, struggle to maintain factual accuracy in such contexts. The study "FreshLLMs: Refreshing Large Language Models with Search Engine Augmentation" addresses these challenges by introducing FreshQA, a dynamic QA benchmark, and FreshPrompt, a method that integrates real-time search engine data into LLM prompts. These innovations aim to enhance the factuality of LLMs and reduce hallucination by providing up-to-date and relevant information. FreshPrompt has shown significant improvements in LLM performance, outperforming other search engine-augmented methods and commercial systems.

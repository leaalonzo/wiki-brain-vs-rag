## Definition
Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning refers to the process of adapting a general-purpose machine learning model, particularly large language models (LLMs), to perform optimally in a specialized field or domain. This involves training the model with domain-specific data and techniques to enhance its performance in tasks relevant to that domain.

## Context
In the context of machine learning and natural language processing, general-purpose models often struggle with tasks in knowledge-intensive domains due to their lack of specialized knowledge. Domain-specific fine-tuning addresses this challenge by incorporating techniques that tailor the model to the nuances of a particular field. For example, in the paper "Customized Retrieval Augmented Generation and Benchmarking for EDA Tool Documentation QA," a customized framework is proposed for electronic design automation (EDA) tool documentation question-answering. This involves fine-tuning text embedding models with EDA-specific knowledge and using a high-quality EDA domain corpus for generative LLMs.

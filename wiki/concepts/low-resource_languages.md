## Definition
Low-resource languages are languages that lack sufficient digital resources, such as large corpora, linguistic tools, and computational models, to support advanced natural language processing (NLP) tasks. These languages often have limited representation in technological and research developments compared to high-resource languages like English.

## Context
The development and application of large language models (LLMs) have predominantly focused on high-resource languages, leaving low-resource languages underrepresented. This disparity is addressed by frameworks like Okapi, which aim to democratize the benefits of LLMs by providing resources and datasets for a diverse set of languages, including those with low resources. Okapi utilizes Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to enhance instruction tuning in 26 languages, emphasizing the inclusion of less studied languages. This approach not only improves the accessibility of LLM technology but also supports the development of NLP applications in low-resource languages.

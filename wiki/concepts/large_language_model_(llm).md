## Definition
A Large Language Model (LLM) is a type of artificial intelligence model designed to understand and generate human-like text. These models are trained on vast amounts of text data and are capable of performing a wide range of language-related tasks, such as translation, summarization, and question-answering.

## Context
Large Language Models have gained prominence due to their ability to process and generate text with a high degree of fluency and coherence. They are used in various applications, from chatbots to automated content creation. The development and fine-tuning of LLMs involve training on diverse datasets to capture linguistic nuances and domain-specific knowledge.

In specialized fields like electronic design automation (EDA), standard LLMs may face challenges due to the lack of domain-specific knowledge. To address this, customized frameworks, such as the one proposed in the paper "Customized Retrieval Augmented Generation and Benchmarking for EDA Tool Documentation QA," enhance LLMs with domain-specific techniques. This includes contrastive learning for better text embedding, reranking methods, and fine-tuning with domain-specific corpora. The paper also introduces the ORD-QA benchmark to evaluate the effectiveness of these customized frameworks in EDA contexts.

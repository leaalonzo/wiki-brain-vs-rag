## Definition
Vision-Language Alignment refers to the process of ensuring that outputs generated by multimodal models, which process both visual and textual data, are accurately grounded in the context provided by both modalities. This alignment is crucial to prevent issues such as hallucinations, where the textual output does not accurately reflect the visual input.

## Context
In the realm of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), vision-language alignment is a significant challenge. Misalignment can lead to outputs that are inconsistent with the visual context, resulting in hallucinations. The paper "Aligning Large Multimodal Models with Factually Augmented RLHF" introduces a novel approach called Factually Augmented Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (Fact-RLHF). This method enhances the traditional RLHF by incorporating factual information, such as image captions, into the reward model to improve alignment. The approach has demonstrated significant improvements in performance on benchmarks like LLaVA-Bench, achieving a 94% performance level, and a 60% improvement on MMHAL-BENCH, a new evaluation benchmark developed to assess real-world scenarios with a focus on penalizing hallucinations. The authors have open-sourced their code, model, and data to facilitate further research and development.

## Definition
Common-sense reasoning refers to the ability of a system to make judgments and inferences that align with human understanding and everyday knowledge. It involves drawing conclusions from implicit information and understanding the context in which statements are made.

## Context
Common-sense reasoning is crucial in various artificial intelligence applications, particularly in natural language processing (NLP), where it aids in understanding and interpreting human language. A notable advancement in this field is the Three-hop Reasoning (THOR) framework, which utilizes chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting to enhance implicit sentiment analysis (ISA). This approach mimics human-like reasoning by inferring latent opinions and aspects, demonstrating substantial improvements in performance, especially in both supervised and zero-shot settings. The THOR framework employs a three-step prompting method and a self-consistency mechanism to ensure the correctness of each reasoning step. This makes it broadly applicable to other NLP problems with minimal effort.

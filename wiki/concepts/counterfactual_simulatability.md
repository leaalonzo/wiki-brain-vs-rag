## Definition
Counterfactual simulatability refers to the ability of a model or system to simulate and provide explanations for its predictions that remain consistent even when hypothetical changes, or counterfactuals, are introduced into the input. It emphasizes the importance of explanations that accurately reflect the model's decision-making process, regardless of alterations in input conditions.

## Context
The concept of counterfactual simulatability is particularly relevant in the evaluation of large language models (LLMs) and their chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities. Recent research has highlighted that while CoT explanations can enhance task performance by offering step-by-step reasoning, they often fail to faithfully represent the model's true reasoning process. This discrepancy becomes evident when biasing features, such as reordering multiple-choice options, are introduced, leading to significant drops in task accuracy. Ensuring counterfactual simulatability is crucial for developing transparent and trustworthy AI systems, as it addresses the need for explanations that remain valid under various hypothetical scenarios.

## Definition
A dynamic QA benchmark is an evaluation framework designed to assess the performance of question-answering systems in scenarios where knowledge is rapidly changing or involves debunking false premises. It tests the factual accuracy and adaptability of language models in real-time, dynamic environments.

## Context
Dynamic QA benchmarks have become crucial due to the limitations of large language models (LLMs) in handling fast-evolving information and misinformation. Traditional static benchmarks may not adequately reflect the challenges posed by current events or newly emerging facts. The FreshQA benchmark, introduced in the study "FreshLLMs: Refreshing Large Language Models with Search Engine Augmentation," exemplifies a dynamic QA benchmark. It features 600 questions categorized into never-changing, slow-changing, fast-changing, and false-premise types, evaluating LLMs on correctness and hallucination through RELAXED and STRICT modes. The study highlights the limitations of current LLMs, especially in handling fast-changing knowledge and false premises, and introduces FreshPrompt, a method that enhances LLM performance by incorporating real-time data from search engines. FreshPrompt significantly improves factuality and reduces hallucination, outperforming other search engine-augmented methods like Self-Ask and commercial systems such as Perplexity.AI. Human evaluations with over 50,000 judgments reveal that LLMs struggle with fast-changing and false-premise questions, regardless of model size. The study commits to regularly updating the FreshQA dataset to encourage ongoing research in improving LLM factuality.

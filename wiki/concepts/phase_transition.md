# Phase Transition

## Definition
A phase transition refers to a fundamental change in the state or properties of a system that occurs at a critical threshold. In the context of physical systems, phase transitions are well-known phenomena, such as the transition from liquid to gas at the boiling point or from solid to liquid at the melting point. These transitions are characterized by abrupt changes in physical properties, such as density or magnetization, and are often driven by changes in external conditions like temperature or pressure.

In cognitive science and artificial intelligence, the concept of phase transition has been metaphorically applied to describe sudden and qualitative changes in system behavior as certain parameters are varied. This is particularly relevant in the study of [[emergent abilities]] in large-scale models, where new capabilities appear suddenly at specific scales.

## Key Mechanisms
Phase transitions in physical systems are typically driven by the collective behavior of a large number of interacting components. For example, the alignment of magnetic spins in a material can lead to a phase transition from a non-magnetic to a magnetic state. In artificial intelligence, phase transitions may occur due to the complex interactions between model parameters, data, and computational resources, leading to the emergence of new abilities or behaviors.

In the context of large language models, phase transitions are observed when a model's performance on a task remains at chance levels until a critical scale is reached, after which performance improves significantly. This suggests that certain capabilities emerge only when the model reaches a sufficient level of complexity or capacity.

## Evidence Base
The paper "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models" by Jason Wei et al. provides evidence for phase transitions in the context of large language models. The authors describe how certain abilities, such as arithmetic tasks, transliteration, and truthfulness in answering questions, emerge unpredictably at specific scales. These emergent abilities are not present in smaller models and cannot be predicted by extrapolating from their performance.

## Connections to Other Concepts
- **[[Emergent Abilities]]**: Phase transitions are closely related to the concept of emergent abilities in large language models, where new capabilities appear at specific scales.
- **[[Scaling Laws]]**: While scaling laws predict gradual improvements in model performance with increased size, phase transitions highlight the sudden and unpredictable emergence of new abilities.
- **[[Few-Shot Prompting]]**: This is a common setting where emergent abilities and phase transitions have been observed, as models demonstrate new capabilities with minimal examples.

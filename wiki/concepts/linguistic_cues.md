## Definition
Linguistic cues are elements within language that provide insight into underlying aspects such as personality, emotion, and psychology. These cues are crucial in understanding and interpreting dialogue, allowing for more nuanced and personalized communication.

## Context
Linguistic cues play a significant role in enhancing dialogue systems, particularly those utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs). The paper "Cue-CoT: Chain-of-thought Prompting for Responding to In-depth Dialogue Questions with LLMs" introduces a novel approach called Cue-CoT, which incorporates intermediate reasoning steps to identify these cues. This method aims to improve the personalization and engagement of responses by considering user status indicators such as personality, emotion, and psychology.

Cue-CoT introduces an intermediate reasoning step to identify linguistic cues in dialogue, enhancing response generation. The approach targets three major linguistic cues: personality, emotion, and psychology. A benchmark with six datasets in Chinese and English was created to evaluate the method. Experiments show that Cue-CoT outperforms standard prompting methods in terms of helpfulness and acceptability. Two variants, O-Cue CoT and M-Cue CoT, were proposed, with M-Cue CoT demonstrating superior robustness and reasoning performance. The study also suggests using intermediate reasoning results for demonstration selection in scenarios with limited training data.

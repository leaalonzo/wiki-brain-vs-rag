## Definition
Few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting is a technique used in large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities by providing a few examples of the reasoning process. This approach allows the model to generate more accurate and coherent responses by mimicking the step-by-step thought process demonstrated in the examples.

## Context
Few-shot CoT prompting is part of a broader set of strategies aimed at improving the reasoning performance of LLMs. It contrasts with zero-shot prompting, where no examples are provided, and the model must rely solely on its pre-trained knowledge. The Few-shot CoT approach has been shown to improve reasoning accuracy by guiding the model through complex tasks with minimal examples.

The paper "Plan-and-Solve Prompting: Improving Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Reasoning by Large Language Models" highlights the limitations of Zero-shot CoT prompting, such as calculation errors, missing-step errors, and semantic misunderstanding errors. It introduces Plan-and-Solve (PS) prompting as an alternative strategy that incorporates planning and executing subtasks, demonstrating performance comparable to few-shot CoT prompting in certain scenarios.

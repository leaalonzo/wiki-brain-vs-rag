## Definition
Large language models (LLMs) are advanced artificial intelligence systems designed to understand and generate human-like text. These models are trained on vast datasets and have billions of parameters, enabling them to perform a wide range of natural language processing tasks, including text generation, translation, summarization, and code-related tasks.

## Context
LLMs have gained prominence for their capabilities in various domains, including software engineering and reasoning tasks. Recent studies highlight the potential of LLMs like CodeX and CodeT5+ in generating test cases for software programs, improving code quality and pass rates on benchmark datasets like HumanEval+ and MBPP.

The study "Towards Understanding Chain-of-Thought Prompting: An Empirical Study of What Matters" explores the effectiveness of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in enhancing the multi-step reasoning abilities of LLMs. It reveals that CoT prompting can maintain high performance even with invalid reasoning steps, suggesting that relevance to the query and correct ordering of reasoning steps are crucial for effective reasoning. The research suggests that LLMs may already possess reasoning abilities from pretraining, with CoT prompting mainly guiding the format and order of reasoning.

Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting is a novel zero-shot chain-of-thought method designed to enhance reasoning capabilities without manually crafted examples. By dividing complex tasks into smaller subtasks and providing detailed instructions, PS Prompting addresses common pitfalls in zero-shot CoT, such as calculation errors, missing reasoning steps, and semantic misunderstandings. It outperforms traditional Zero-shot-CoT and is competitive with few-shot CoT methods across various reasoning tasks. The PS+ Prompting extension further improves reasoning quality by offering detailed instructions to address calculation errors. PS+ prompting achieves performance similar to 8-shot CoT prompting in arithmetic reasoning tasks without requiring manual examples.

However, limitations exist in CoT reasoning. The study "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought" reveals that CoT explanations can be systematically unfaithful to the true reasoning process. Biasing features in inputs can lead to misleading rationalizations, posing risks for transparency and safety in AI systems. On social-bias tasks, LLMs may justify biased answers without acknowledging stereotypes, leading to significant accuracy drops. Improving transparency and explainability may require enhancing CoT faithfulness or exploring alternative methods.

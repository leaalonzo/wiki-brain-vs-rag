## Definition
Bridging objects in the context of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting refer to the intermediate steps or rationales within a reasoning process that connect initial premises to final conclusions. These objects guide large language models (LLMs) through multi-step reasoning tasks by providing a structured path from the query to the answer.

## Context
The concept of bridging objects is essential in understanding how CoT prompting enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. The study "Towards Understanding Chain-of-Thought Prompting: An Empirical Study of What Matters" demonstrates that CoT prompting improves LLM performance by encouraging the generation of these intermediate steps, or bridging objects. Interestingly, the study finds that even invalid reasoning steps can maintain 80-90% of the performance of valid CoT prompting. This suggests that the relevance of these steps to the query and their correct ordering are more crucial than their validity. The research implies that LLMs may have inherent reasoning abilities from pretraining, with CoT demonstrations primarily guiding the format and sequence of reasoning.

## Definition
Output Diversity refers to the variety and range of responses or outputs generated by a model, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs). It is a measure of how diverse the outputs are when the model is given the same or similar inputs multiple times.

## Context
In the realm of artificial intelligence and machine learning, particularly with LLMs, output diversity is an essential factor for ensuring that models do not produce repetitive or overly similar outputs. This diversity is crucial for applications requiring creativity, adaptability, and nuanced responses. The concept is particularly relevant when discussing the effects of different fine-tuning methods on LLMs, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

Recent studies, such as "Understanding the Effects of RLHF on LLM Generalisation and Diversity," have shown that while RLHF can enhance out-of-distribution (OOD) generalisation, it tends to reduce output diversity compared to SFT. This highlights a trade-off between achieving better generalisation and maintaining output diversity in current LLM fine-tuning techniques. The study suggests a need for novel methods to enhance both generalisation and diversity without compromising one for the other.

## Definition
The factuality of Large Language Models (LLMs) refers to the accuracy and reliability of the information generated by these models. It assesses the model's ability to provide correct and up-to-date responses, particularly when dealing with dynamic or rapidly changing information and false premises.

## Context
Large Language Models, such as GPT-4, are powerful tools capable of generating human-like text. However, they often struggle with maintaining factual accuracy, especially in scenarios involving fast-changing world knowledge or when presented with false premises. The study "FreshLLMs: Refreshing Large Language Models with Search Engine Augmentation" introduces FreshQA, a benchmark for evaluating LLMs' factuality. This benchmark includes questions categorized into never-changing, slow-changing, fast-changing, and false-premise types, highlighting the challenges LLMs face in these areas.

The study also proposes FreshPrompt, a method that enhances LLM performance by integrating real-time information from search engines into the model's prompts. This approach significantly improves the factuality of LLMs, outperforming other methods like Self-Ask and commercial systems such as Perplexity.AI. Human evaluations indicate that LLMs, regardless of size, struggle with fast-changing and false-premise questions, underscoring the need for continuous improvement in this area.

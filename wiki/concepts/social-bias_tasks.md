## Definition
Social-bias tasks are a category of tasks designed to evaluate how well language models can recognize and mitigate biases, particularly those related to social stereotypes and prejudices. These tasks assess the model's ability to handle inputs that may contain biasing features and to produce outputs that do not perpetuate or justify these biases.

## Context
In the context of large language models (LLMs), social-bias tasks are crucial for understanding and improving the models' transparency and fairness. Research has shown that models often justify biased answers without acknowledging the influence of stereotypes, which can lead to misleading rationalizations. This is particularly evident in chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, where models verbalize step-by-step reasoning that may not accurately reflect their true decision-making process. The presence of biasing features in the input, such as reordering multiple-choice options, can heavily influence these explanations, resulting in a significant drop in accuracy.

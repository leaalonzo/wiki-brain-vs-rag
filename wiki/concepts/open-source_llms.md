## Definition
Open-source large language models (LLMs) are advanced artificial intelligence systems designed to understand and generate human-like text. These models are developed and released with open-source licenses, allowing researchers and developers to access, modify, and distribute the underlying code and data freely.

## Context
Open-source LLMs have gained significant attention due to their potential to democratize access to powerful language technologies. Traditionally, many LLMs have been primarily tuned for English and a few other widely spoken languages. However, recent advancements, such as the introduction of Okapi, have expanded the capabilities of open-source LLMs to support multiple languages. Okapi is a framework designed to enhance LLMs through instruction tuning in 26 languages, including less studied and low-resource languages like Telugu, Ukrainian, Nepali, and Kannada. It utilizes Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for instruction-tuning, which generally outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in multilingual settings. Okapi uses BLOOM and LLaMA as base pre-trained models and provides resources such as instruction datasets and response ranking data, available on GitHub, to support further research and development.

## Definition
Multi-step reasoning refers to the cognitive process of solving problems or answering questions by breaking them down into a series of intermediate steps or rationales. This approach is often used to tackle complex tasks that require sequential logic and decision-making.

## Context
The concept of multi-step reasoning is particularly relevant in the field of artificial intelligence, especially in the development and enhancement of large language models (LLMs). Recent studies, such as "Towards Understanding Chain-of-Thought Prompting: An Empirical Study of What Matters," have explored techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to improve the multi-step reasoning capabilities of LLMs. CoT prompting involves guiding models to generate intermediate reasoning steps, which can significantly enhance their problem-solving abilities.

The study highlights that while the validity of individual reasoning steps is less critical, the relevance to the query and the correct ordering of these steps are crucial for effective reasoning. This suggests that LLMs may already possess inherent reasoning abilities from pretraining, with CoT demonstrations primarily serving to guide the format and order of the output. The research also opens new questions about LLMs' ability to learn reasoning in context and challenges the evaluation of few-shot reasoning.
